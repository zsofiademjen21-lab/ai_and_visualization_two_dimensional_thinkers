What we found: 7 key takeaways

Takeaway 1

The model often fails to recognise disturbed galaxies

Examples: disturbed galaxies the model got wrong

Takeaway 2 tested by editing images

When a galaxy loses detail, the model calls it an elliptical

Examples: the same spiral before and after shrinking (top) or blurring (bottom)

Why: without detail, the arms disappear and the galaxy looks like a smooth blob, which is what an elliptical looks like.

Takeaway 3 tested by editing images

Small, harmless changes flip the answer for disturbed galaxies

Examples: the same disturbed galaxy, just rotated by 90°

Takeaway 4

The model memorised the rare classes instead of learning them

Takeaway 5

The model is often very sure even when it is wrong

Examples: the model's most confident mistakes (true label → model's answer)

Are the labels wrong? Checked with the volunteers' own votes. For the 202 mistakes (validation and training) whose Galaxy Zoo votes are public (about 45% of galaxies), the votes back the label in 54% of cases, the volunteers were split in 18%, both answers fit in 17%, and the votes back the model in only 10%. So wrong labels explain only about 1 in 10 mistakes. When the model calls a disturbed galaxy a spiral, it is usually a disturbed spiral: most volunteers saw both the disturbance and the spiral arms, so the five classes force one answer for a galaxy that is both.

On the map of the model's answers, mistakes sit in two kinds of places

Takeaway 7 tested by editing images

What does not confuse the model

Some things looked like they might matter, so we changed them on purpose. The answers barely moved.

Colour is the important one: in the real images, red disturbed galaxies are called Elliptical far more often than blue ones, so it looked like the model judges by colour. The test shows it doesn't. Red disturbed galaxies are probably just smoother in shape.

Data: Galaxy10 DECals validation images (not used for training). "Tested by editing images": we changed one thing in the images (size, blur, colour, shape, rotation, position or neighbours) and ran the model again on the same galaxies (705 and 1,115 galaxies in two tests).
