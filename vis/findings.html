What we found: 5 key takeaways

Takeaway 1

The model often fails to recognise disturbed galaxies

Examples: disturbed galaxies the model got wrong

Takeaway 2 tested by editing images

Small galaxies get mistaken for ellipticals

Examples: the same spiral, before and after shrinking it

Why: in a small image the arms or disturbed structure disappear, so the galaxy looks like a smooth blob, which is what an elliptical looks like.

Takeaway 3 tested by editing images

Colour is not the reason, even though it looks like it

Examples: red disturbed galaxies made bluer, and the model's answer stays the same

Takeaway 4 tested by editing images

Just rotating the image changes many answers for disturbed galaxies

Examples: the same galaxy, rotated by 90°

Takeaway 5

The model is often very sure even when it is wrong

Examples: the model's most confident mistakes (true label → model's answer)

Still to check: some of these may be wrong labels, not model mistakes. At a first look, about 16 of the 40 most confident mistakes seem mislabelled, mostly "disturbed" galaxies that look like normal spirals, or "spirals" that are clearly two galaxies merging. We need to check this by hand.

Data: Galaxy10 DECals validation images (not used for training). "Tested by editing images": we changed one thing in the images (size, colour or rotation) and ran the model again on 705 galaxies. Numbers are calculated live from the model's predictions. For details, open explorer.html.
